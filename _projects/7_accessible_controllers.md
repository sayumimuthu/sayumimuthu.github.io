**Area:** Assistive technology, neuroengineering &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Period:** July – December 2024 &nbsp;·&nbsp; **Where:** [NeuroBionics Laboratory](https://biomedical.eng.unimelb.edu.au/john-neurobionics), University of Melbourne

Standard game controllers can be difficult or impossible to use for children with cerebral palsy. During my research internship, I worked on an assistive-technology project exploring **accessible gaming controllers** for these children.

#### What I did

- Contributed to an assistive-technology project exploring accessible gaming controllers for children with cerebral palsy.
- Explored **EEG signal characteristics** for **brain-computer interfacing (BCI)** applications.
