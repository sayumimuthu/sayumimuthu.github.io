Hi, I’m Sayumi! I am a Research Assistant at the Multidisciplinary AI Research Centre (MARC), University of Peradeniya, where I am the lead research assistant for the Generative AI – Large Language Model (LLM) research group and a research assistant in the Generative AI – Genetics group. I graduated from the University of Peradeniya in 2025 with a B.Sc. in Computer Engineering with First Class Honours.

My research interests are in natural language processing, responsible AI, large language models and computational biology, and their applications, mostly in biomedical engineering. I am particularly interested in making language models more reliable, fair and safe to use, especially in health-related settings. My current work spans:

  • Trustworthy LLM evaluation: separating genuine prompt sensitivity from artifacts of heuristic scoring and LLM-as-judge evaluation (project).
  • Fairness and social bias: how gender stereotypes surface in LLM-generated children’s stories, and whether model hidden states causally drive them (project).
  • Hallucination and factuality: using internal representations and energy-based signals to detect and mitigate hallucinations (project).
  • LLMs in mental health: whether conversational LLMs recognize depression-related cues, in collaboration with the Department of Psychiatry, University of Illinois Chicago (project).
  • Generative models for single-cell genomics: absorbing-state discrete diffusion for scRNA-seq imputation (project).

Before joining MARC, I was a research intern at the NeuroBionics Laboratory, University of Melbourne, where I contributed to accessible gaming controllers for children with cerebral palsy and explored EEG signals for brain-computer interfaces. My final-year undergraduate research was on classifying single-joint motor-imagery EEG tasks. I have also taught undergraduate laboratories and tutorials in the Department of Computer Engineering since 2023.

  1. MERCon
    Prompt Sensitivity or Evaluation Artifact? A Task-Aware Analysis for Large Language Models
    Sayumi Muthukumarana, Buddhi Wijenayake, Roshan Godaliyadda, and Parakrama Ekanayake
    In 2026 12th IEEE Moratuwa Engineering Research Conference (MERCon), Moratuwa, Sri Lanka, Aug 2026
    Abs Bib HTML Code

    Is the variation in LLM outputs across prompt templates a property of the model, or partly an artifact of how responses are scored? We design an eight-template evaluation experiment spanning ARC-Challenge, BoolQ and SQuAD, covering nine LLMs and 43,200 prompt-response-judge triples. We introduce Evaluation-Attributable Sensitivity (EAS) and Signed EAS to separate genuine generation variation from differences introduced by heuristic scoring and LLM-as-judge evaluation, and examine how benchmark format and scoring method affect observed sensitivity, identifying contexts in which automatic metrics under- or over-estimate response instability.

    My contribution: Conceptualization; formulation and analysis of evaluation-attributable sensitivity across controlled LLM experiments; quantitative evaluation; writing (original draft, review and editing).

  2. arXiv
    Once Upon a Bias: Gender Stereotyping in LLM-Generated Children’s Stories
    Sayumi Muthukumarana, Roshan Godaliyadda, Parakrama Ekanayake, and Roshan Ragel
    arXiv preprint, 2026
    Abs Bib Code

    We study how the gender of an intended child and the identity of the narrator shape character traits and social-role portrayals in children’s stories generated by large language models. Using matched daughter-son comparisons, we combine agency/communion and trait lexicons, matched interaction analyses and human assessments of narrative differences. Through probing and activation interventions, we further examine whether recipient information encoded in model hidden states has a causal influence on generated portrayals, distinguishing decodability from demonstrated causation.

    My contribution: Conceptualization; experimental design; dataset curation; quantitative evaluation; writing (original draft, review and editing).

  3. ICLR
    ABSORB: Absorbing-State Discrete Diffusion for Single-Cell Gene Expression Imputation
    Chandula Adhikari, Sayumi Muthukumarana, Roshan Godaliyadda, and Parakrama Ekanayake
    2026
    Under review at ICLR 2027
    Abs

    Zero counts in single-cell RNA sequencing are ambiguous: some reflect missing transcript measurements (dropout), others are biologically meaningful. ABSORB treats dropout as masked missingness and performs selective imputation with an absorbing [MASK]-state discrete diffusion formulation, using per-gene quantile tokenization, a role-conditioned Transformer, fixed observed context and confidence-ordered parallel reconstruction.

    My contribution: Research on modeling dropout as masked missingness; experimental evaluation; writing (original draft, review and editing).
